## Correction to my earlier concern 1 — I was wrong, and the mutant proves it

My original report said: *"Mutating `if req.get("limit"):` → `is not None` would
leave the suite green: no test resumes a session whose persisted `limit` is 0."*

**That claim was false, and I had not measured it.** I reasoned it from the
absence of a *resume* test with `limit: 0` and did not consider that `_get_query`
now routes through the same helper with its own default `limit=0`. The mutant
reddens **22 tests**. The fork was never silent.

This is precisely the error my own dispatch warned about — asserting a
constraint's status without mutating for it — and I made it in the sentence
where I was flagging someone else's version of it. Recording it plainly rather
than quietly letting the new test's arrival paper over it.

**The test is still worth having, and the ruling still stands, for a different
reason than I gave.** The 21 collateral failures would catch the regression, but
every one of them fails somewhere downstream — `FileNotFoundError`, a missing
`"FAILED"` in output, an empty shortlist — with nothing pointing at the limit
stamp. A reader who "corrected" the clause would see the pipeline collapse and
have no idea why. The new test fails *at* the fork, on a one-line assertion,
with a docstring naming the reason. That is the value: not catching the
regression, but **diagnosing** it. The commit message says so in those terms.
